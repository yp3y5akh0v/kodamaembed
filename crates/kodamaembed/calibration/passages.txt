The speed of light in a vacuum is about 299,792 kilometers per second, and nothing that carries information can travel faster. Ocean tides are caused mainly by the gravitational pull of the Moon, with a smaller contribution from the Sun. The Moon pulls slightly harder on the side of Earth facing it than on the far side, which stretches the oceans into two bulges. As the Earth rotates, most coastlines pass through both bulges each day, producing two high tides and two low tides. When the Sun and Moon line up during new and full moons, their effects combine to create especially large spring tides. Local geography matters a great deal: some bays funnel the water so strongly that the difference between high and low tide exceeds ten meters.

Honeybees communicate the location of food through a behavior known as the waggle dance. A forager returning to the hive moves in a figure eight pattern, and during the straight middle section she waggles her body from side to side. The angle of that straight run relative to vertical indicates the direction of the food relative to the sun, and the length of the waggle conveys the distance. Other bees follow the dancer closely, sensing her movements in the dark hive, and then fly out to find the source themselves. This remarkable system allows a colony to direct its workforce to the richest flowers within several kilometers.
